# Professor Sipe style calibration

The seeded Trademark bank uses provisional style calibration appropriate to Professor Sipe's course. No current-course Trademark exam or model answer is bundled here, so do not claim exact exam-format fidelity.

Preserve these style dimensions unless newer direct course evidence changes them:

- concise but fact-dense applications;
- issue-concealing stems that require the learner to identify the governing doctrine;
- close distractors built from neighboring tests, proof errors, or right-result/wrong-reason analysis;
- precise alignment between the claimed mark or trade dress and the evidence offered;
- attention to proof quality, survey population, primary significance, and the difference between commercial success and source significance;
- original products, parties, and factual engines rather than disguised assigned cases or prior practice questions.

Subject-matter firewall: only Sabrina's current Trademark syllabus, assigned readings, posted slides, handouts, and notes may control doctrine. Prior Sipe materials from another course may inform pacing and architecture only and may not add Trademark law.

Revisit this provisional calibration if Professor Sipe supplies a current Trademark practice question, exam, rubric, or model answer.

## Professor Makar (Constitutional Criminal Procedure I) style calibration

No current-course Con Crim Pro exam or model answer is bundled here. Four hypothetical practice sets exist in `sources/con-crim-pro/` and are treated strictly as a factual blacklist and a loose guide to Prof. Makar's preference for sequenced, multi-scenario fact patterns — never as a source of doctrine or as material to imitate closely enough to reproduce a distinctive fact pattern.

Preserve these style dimensions unless newer direct course evidence changes them:

- issue-concealing stems built around a short, concrete factual scenario rather than an abstract statement of the rule;
- close distractors built from neighboring or partially-overlapping doctrines (e.g., trespass theory vs. privacy-expectation theory; search vs. seizure);
- attention to the specific triggering facts that separate one line of cases from a neighboring one (e.g., consent before vs. after possession; ordinary vs. not-generally-available technology);
- original scenarios and settings, never the professor's own hypothetical locales or fact patterns.

Subject-matter firewall: only the current Con Crim Pro I syllabus, assigned casebook pages, posted slides, and class notes may control doctrine.

## Professor Higginbotham (Constitutional Law II) style calibration

No current-course exam or model answer is bundled here. The syllabus states the final exam mixes traditional hypothetical essay questions and multiple-choice questions, and that multiple-choice questions receive no partial credit — consistent with the app's single-best-answer format.

Preserve these style dimensions unless newer direct course evidence changes them:

- concise doctrinal framing tied to a short fact pattern or a paraphrased (unnamed) case scenario;
- distractors that misstate a real doctrinal line (e.g., collapsing a two-prong test into one prong, or inverting which classifications trigger heightened review);
- avoid naming decided cases directly in the stem or choices, consistent with the app's own telegraphing rule, even though the professor's own exam may name cases directly.

Subject-matter firewall: only the current Con Law II syllabus and the student's session notes may control doctrine until slides, handouts, or practice materials are shared.
