## clii-02-q01
Course: Constitutional Law II
Topic: Rationale for heightened scrutiny of race
Set: CLII-02
Coverage: Session 3 (Aug. 25, 2026)
Theme: Driftwood County Civic Center
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 1
Prompt: A court is asked why racial classifications receive markedly more skeptical review than ordinary economic or social classifications. Which combination of considerations best reflects the accepted justification?
A: The original purpose animating the Fourteenth Amendment, the stigma such classifications impose, the practical difficulty of detecting and correcting race-based harms (including unconscious bias), and the difficulty racial minorities face building effective political coalitions.
B: Racial classifications should receive no more scrutiny than any other classification, since all classifications burden someone.
C: Only the size of the burdened population matters; larger groups automatically receive more protection than smaller ones.
D: Heightened scrutiny for race exists solely because the text of the Fourteenth Amendment lists race by name.
Answer: A
Authorities: Rationale factors for heightened scrutiny of race
Explanation: The heightened suspicion applied to racial classifications rests on a cluster of considerations: the amendment's animating purpose, the stigmatic harm of race-based lines, the practical difficulty of policing such harms given unconscious bias, and the political-process difficulties racial minorities can face. B denies any special justification exists, C substitutes a single population-size metric for the actual rationale, and D overstates the role of bare text, since the Fourteenth Amendment does not itself specify tiers of scrutiny.

## clii-02-q02
Course: Constitutional Law II

Set: CLII-02

Theme: Driftwood County Civic Center
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 2
Prompt: A reviewing court applies rational basis review to an ordinary economic regulation. Under that framework, on which of the following grounds may the court strike the law down?
A: Only if the classification has no rational connection to a legitimate purpose, or if the government's actual purpose is itself illegitimate; the court also extends a presumption favoring the facts underlying the classification and defers to a reasonably conceivable legislative judgment.
B: Only if the plaintiff can show the legislature considered no other options.
C: Only if a majority of the affected class opposes the law politically.
D: Only if the law imposes any cost at all on a private party.
Answer: A
Authorities: Two grounds for striking legislation under rational basis; presumption/deference framework
Explanation: Under rational basis review, a law falls only if the classification lacks any rational connection to a legitimate purpose or if the underlying purpose is itself illegitimate, and courts presume the facts favorable to the government while deferring to any reasonably conceivable justification. B, C, and D each substitute a standard the deferential framework does not use.

## clii-02-q03
Course: Constitutional Law II
Topic: Step-by-step regulation and selective enforcement
Set: CLII-02
Coverage: Session 2 (Aug. 20, 2026)
Theme: Driftwood County Civic Center
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 3
Prompt: A city regulates only large commercial delivery trucks operating downtown, leaving smaller commercial vehicles and passenger cars unregulated for now, citing downtown congestion. An affected trucking company argues the city is unfairly singling it out. Under rational basis review's tolerance for incremental regulation, what must the company show to succeed with that argument?
A: That the company simply dislikes being regulated first.
B: That large trucks do not belong in the congestion-causing category at all, since a government may otherwise address a problem step by step without regulating every contributor at once.
C: That large trucks contribute in some way to downtown congestion, which would defeat the claim.
D: That the city could have chosen a completely different regulatory tool.
Answer: B
Authorities: Step-by-step regulation and the 'picking on me' objection
Explanation: A 'why are you picking on me' objection fails under rational basis review so long as the regulated party actually belongs in the category the government is addressing, since government may regulate a problem incrementally rather than comprehensively; succeeding requires showing the party does not belong in the category at all. A is not a legal argument, C actually defeats the company's own claim rather than supporting it, and D asks for a least-restrictive-alternative showing rational basis does not require.

Title: Question 4
Prompt: An early Supreme Court decision concluded that a formerly enslaved person could not be a citizen entitled to sue in federal court, reasoning in part from what the framers of the founding document were understood to have intended at the time. What is the central problem with resting a citizenship determination on that kind of original-intent reasoning here?
A: Original-intent reasoning is always an illegitimate method of constitutional interpretation in every context.
B: The reasoning is unproblematic because original intent is the only permissible interpretive method.
C: It uses the framers' own exclusionary assumptions about who counted as part of the political community to justify perpetuating that same exclusion, rather than independently examining whether those assumptions were themselves defensible.
D: The problem is purely procedural: the case should have been heard in state court rather than federal court.
Answer: C
Authorities: Original-intent and citizenship reasoning in historical due-process cases
Explanation: The deeper problem is treating the framers' own exclusionary assumptions as an unexamined premise for resolving present citizenship questions, rather than asking whether those assumptions could be defended at all. A takes an absolute position broader than the criticism requires, B simply endorses the flawed reasoning, and D substitutes a jurisdictional technicality for the substantive criticism.

## clii-02-q05
Course: Constitutional Law II
Topic: Social construction of race in state-mandated separation
Set: CLII-02
Coverage: Session 3 (Aug. 25, 2026)
Theme: Driftwood County Civic Center
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 5
Prompt: A decision upholding state-mandated racial separation in public accommodations reasoned that any stigma from separation existed only because the excluded group chose to interpret it that way, and that a property-based objection to being assigned to a particular car was properly remedied, if at all, by money damages rather than by invalidating the assignment. What does this reasoning illustrate about how the decision treated race and social meaning?
A: The decision treated racial classification and its social meaning as objective, fixed facts entirely outside of law, immune from any judicial role in constructing or reinforcing them.
B: The decision held that separation could never be challenged under any theory, including a political-rights theory.
C: The decision explicitly acknowledged the state's role in creating the racial distinction and apologized for it in the opinion.
D: The decision treated the stigma of separation as if it were simply a matter of the excluded group's own subjective interpretation, while treating the practical remedy for misassignment as a market-style compensation question rather than a dignitary one, in effect naturalizing a state-created racial distinction rather than acknowledging the state's role in constructing it.
Answer: D
Authorities: Social-construction-of-race reasoning in state-mandated separation
Explanation: By attributing stigma to the excluded group's own perception and treating misassignment as a matter for money damages, the decision obscured the state's own active role in constructing and enforcing the racial line, presenting a state-created distinction as though it were a natural or neutral fact. A overstates the claim into a sweeping immunity thesis the facts do not support, B ignores that the decision did engage with (and reject) certain theories rather than foreclosing all of them, and C contradicts the facts described.

Title: Question 6
Prompt: A decision holding that state-mandated separate schools violate equal protection is sometimes described narrowly, as though it applies only to public education and only where facilities are demonstrably unequal. Which description more accurately captures the actual reach of that holding?
A: The holding is limited strictly to public elementary schools and does not extend to any other level of public education.
B: The holding prohibits government-imposed separation of the races across contexts, not merely education, and does not depend on a further showing that facilities were unequal, since state-imposed separation is treated as constitutionally problematic even when facilities are nominally equal; but it is limited to state action.
C: The holding applies to private conduct as readily as to government conduct, since the harm to the excluded student is the same either way.
D: The holding applies only where a plaintiff separately proves financial harm from the separation.
Answer: B
Authorities: Scope of school-desegregation holding: state action and all-aspects reach
Explanation: The holding's principle reaches government-imposed separation broadly, not merely education, and does not turn on proving unequal facilities, since state-mandated separation is itself the constitutional problem; at the same time, it is expressly limited to state action, not private conduct. A wrongly narrows it to one educational level, C wrongly extends it to private actors, and D adds a financial-harm requirement the holding does not impose.

## clii-02-q07
Course: Constitutional Law II
Topic: Categorical race-based exclusion and available defenses
Set: CLII-02
Coverage: Session 3 (Aug. 25, 2026)
Theme: Driftwood County Civic Center
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 7
Prompt: A state statute categorically bars a racial group from serving on any jury. The state defends the statute by arguing that the excluded group suffered no cognizable harm, because no specific excluded individual can prove that a jury without them would have decided any particular case differently. Which response most directly refutes that defense, consistent with why such categorical exclusions are treated as constitutionally distinct from ordinary eligibility rules?
A: The defense succeeds, because without proof of a different outcome in a specific case there is no constitutional harm at all.
B: The defense succeeds because jury service is a privilege, not a right, and privileges receive no equal protection scrutiny.
C: The harm targeted by equal protection in this context is the legal discrimination and stamp of inferiority worked by the categorical exclusion itself, which does not depend on proving that any individual case would have come out differently.
D: The only relevant harm is the lost wages excluded jurors would have earned from jury service.
Answer: C
Authorities: Categorical race-based exclusion from civic participation
Explanation: The constitutional harm from a categorical racial exclusion from jury service is the legal discrimination and stigmatic inferiority the exclusion itself works, independent of whether any specific case's outcome can be shown to have differed. A and B both accept the state's outcome-based or privilege-based defense despite the doctrine rejecting exactly that framing, and D trivializes the harm into a narrow economic loss.
