## demo-a-q01
Course: Demo Course A
Topic: Notice and response
Set: DEMO-A
Coverage: Fictional demonstration source, items 1-4
Theme: Lakeshore Licensing Board
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 1
Prompt: A licensing board sends Kai a notice stating only that "concerns have arisen." At the hearing, the board relies on an undisclosed allegation that Kai altered inspection photos and immediately revokes the license. Kai had no earlier opportunity to address that allegation. Under the assigned demonstration source, which conclusion is strongest?
A: The process is deficient because Kai lacked notice of the specific issue and a meaningful opportunity to respond before the final penalty.
B: The process is sufficient because any notice that concerns exist permits reliance on every possible allegation.
C: The process is sufficient because an opportunity to appeal always substitutes for a pre-penalty response.
D: The process is deficient only if Kai proves the board would certainly have reached a different result.
Answer: A
Authorities: Fictional demonstration source, Demo Course A item 1
Explanation: Rule. The fictional source requires notice of the specific issue and a meaningful opportunity to respond before a final penalty. Application. The vague notice did not identify altered photographs, and the board imposed the penalty before Kai could answer that allegation. Why the other choices fail. B treats generic notice as specific, C invents automatic substitution by appeal, and D adds a certain-outcome requirement.

## demo-a-q02
Course: Demo Course A
Topic: Informed waiver
Set: DEMO-A
Coverage: Fictional demonstration source, items 1-4
Theme: Lakeshore Licensing Board
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 2
Prompt: Mira is offered an immediate informal conference or a later recorded hearing. An administrator asks Mira to sign "I choose the conference" but does not explain that the conference waives testimony and review rights or that the recorded hearing remains available. Under the assigned demonstration source, what is the best analysis?
A: The signature is sufficient because every written choice is informed.
B: The waiver is ineffective because Mira was not given the material consequences and available alternative before choosing.
C: The waiver is effective unless Mira can prove the administrator intended deception.
D: The waiver is effective because an informal procedure can never affect a material right.
Answer: B
Authorities: Fictional demonstration source, Demo Course A item 2
Explanation: Rule. The fictional source permits waiver only after disclosure of material consequences and available alternatives. Application. Mira received neither the lost rights nor the available recorded-hearing alternative. Why the other choices fail. A makes a signature conclusive, C adds an intent requirement, and D assumes away the stated consequences.

## demo-a-q03
Course: Demo Course A
Topic: Allocation of decisions
Set: DEMO-A
Coverage: Fictional demonstration source, items 1-4
Theme: Lakeshore Licensing Board
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 3
Prompt: A representative believes admitting the violation will minimize a penalty. The participant repeatedly directs the representative to contest liability, while authorizing the representative to choose witnesses and organize the presentation. Under the assigned demonstration source, who controls the decision whether to admit the violation?
A: The representative, because selecting any hearing position is tactical.
B: The representative, whenever the proposed admission is objectively sensible.
C: The participant, because admitting liability changes the ultimate objective rather than merely selecting ordinary tactical means.
D: The board, because disagreement automatically transfers control to the decision maker.
Answer: C
Authorities: Fictional demonstration source, Demo Course A item 3
Explanation: Rule. The fictional source allocates ordinary tactical means to the representative but reserves the ultimate objective to the participant. Application. Whether to contest or admit liability defines the participant's objective; witness and presentation choices are means. Why the other choices fail. A and B relabel the objective as tactics, and D invents automatic board control.

## demo-a-q04
Course: Demo Course A
Topic: Authorized information sharing
Set: DEMO-A
Coverage: Fictional demonstration source, items 1-4
Theme: Lakeshore Licensing Board
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 4
Prompt: A participant authorizes a representative to seek reinstatement but expressly directs that a pending job offer remain undisclosed. The representative thinks telling the board about the offer would make reinstatement more likely. Under the assigned demonstration source, may the representative disclose it without further permission?
A: Yes, because any useful information is automatically authorized for disclosure.
B: Yes, because the board is entitled to every fact known to the representative.
C: No, unless the board first promises not to consider the information adversely.
D: No, because the participant's specific instruction limits authorization; the representative needs informed permission or another source-supported basis.
Answer: D
Authorities: Fictional demonstration source, Demo Course A item 4
Explanation: Rule. The fictional source allows information sharing only when authorized as reasonably necessary or through informed permission, and a specific instruction limits ordinary authorization. Application. The participant expressly withheld the job offer, so tactical usefulness does not authorize disclosure. Why the other choices fail. A ignores the restriction, B invents a board entitlement, and C substitutes the recipient's promise for participant permission.
