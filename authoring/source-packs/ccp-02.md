## ccp-02-q01
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Seizure of a person and property
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 1-3 (Aug. 17-24, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: medium
Type: mc
Title: Question 1
Prompt: An officer signals a driver to pull over for a broken taillight. The driver stops. The officer shines a flashlight through the window to look at the back seat but touches nothing and opens no compartment. Which characterization is most accurate?
A: Both the driver and the vehicle have been seized by the stop, and the flashlight look is not itself a search of anything inside the passenger compartment.
B: Neither the driver nor the vehicle has been seized, because a traffic stop is not a meaningful interference with any possessory interest.
C: The vehicle has been searched by the flashlight look, and the driver has additionally been searched by the order to stop.
D: Only the vehicle has been seized; the driver's person is never seized during a routine traffic stop.
Answer: A
Authorities: Seizure definition; search/seizure independence
Explanation: A traffic stop is a meaningful interference with the driver's freedom of movement and the vehicle's possessory use, so both are seized; merely looking into a space in plain view from a lawful vantage point is not itself a search. B denies that a stop is a seizure at all, C mislabels the flashlight look as a search of the interior rather than mere observation, and D wrongly excludes the driver's person from the seizure.

## ccp-02-q02
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Search and seizure as independent inquiries
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 1-3 (Aug. 17-24, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 2
Prompt: A postal inspector, suspicious of a package, opens it, dips a finger into a small amount of white residue, and smells it before resealing the package. Handling the package this way delays its delivery by one full day. Which analysis best separates the two potential constitutional events?
A: Because the package was resealed, no search or seizure occurred at all.
B: Opening and examining the package's contents is the search; whether the one-day delivery delay is a seizure turns on whether it is a meaningful interference with the owner's possessory interest, a separate question from whether a search occurred.
C: The delay is the search, and testing the residue is the seizure.
D: Any physical contact with mail automatically constitutes both a search and a seizure of the same severity.
Answer: B
Authorities: Search/seizure independence; postal package analysis
Explanation: Examining the contents is a search regardless of whether anything is kept, while a seizure asks separately whether the government meaningfully interfered with the owner's possessory interest — here, a full day's delay is the kind of fact that could supply a seizure even though the tiny amount of residue removed would not. A ignores that resealing does not undo an examination that already happened, C swaps which event maps to which concept, and D treats the two inquiries as if they must always rise and fall together.

## ccp-02-q03
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Elements of a valid warrant
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 1-3 (Aug. 17-24, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: medium
Type: mc
Title: Question 3
Prompt: A magistrate issues a warrant supported by a properly sworn affidavit establishing probable cause, but the warrant itself authorizes a search of "the residence and any outbuildings believed to be used by the suspect," without any street address, lot description, or other means of identifying which residence is meant. Which requirement does this warrant most clearly fail?
A: Probable cause, because the affidavit's showing was insufficient.
B: Oath or affirmation, because the affiant did not swear to the facts.
C: Particularity, because an executing officer could not, with reasonable effort, ascertain and identify the intended place from the warrant's description.
D: No requirement is violated, because probable cause alone is sufficient to validate any warrant.
Answer: C
Authorities: Three requirements for a valid warrant
Explanation: A valid warrant needs probable cause, an oath or affirmation, and a description particular enough that an executing officer can identify the place with reasonable effort; a description with no address or other identifying detail fails that third requirement even though probable cause and the oath are not in question here. A and B misattribute the defect to elements the facts do not challenge, and D ignores that particularity is an independent requirement, not subsumed by probable cause.

## ccp-02-q04
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I

Set: CCP-02

Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 4
Prompt: A defendant seeks to challenge a warrant by showing that the supporting affidavit included a statement the affiant knew to be false. Even if the defendant makes a substantial preliminary showing that the false statement was included knowingly, what additional showing determines whether the warrant is nonetheless valid?
A: Whether the affiant apologized for the error once it was discovered.
B: Whether the false statement was material to any element of the underlying offense.
C: Whether the defendant can show the affiant intended to harm the defendant specifically.
D: Whether a neutral magistrate could still find probable cause once the false statement is set aside, in which case the warrant survives.
Answer: D
Authorities: Challenging warrants for false statements
Explanation: Even a knowing or reckless false statement does not invalidate a warrant if probable cause can still be found once that statement is excised from the analysis; the false statement must have been necessary to the probable-cause finding. A is irrelevant to the legal standard, B substitutes an unrelated materiality-to-the-offense inquiry, and C adds a specific-intent-to-harm element the standard does not require.

## ccp-02-q05
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Reasonableness of factual errors in a warrant
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 1-3 (Aug. 17-24, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 5
Prompt: Officers execute a warrant at the address listed, reasonably believing at the time that only one residence occupied that address. It later turns out, unknown to the officers when they applied for and executed the warrant, that the building had been divided into two separate units. Which principle governs whether the search remains valid?
A: The search's validity is judged by what the officers reasonably knew at the time of the search, not by facts revealed only in hindsight.
B: The search is automatically invalid because the warrant did not, in hindsight, accurately describe the property.
C: The search is valid only if the officers can prove the division was fraudulently concealed from them.
D: The search is automatically valid because officers are never responsible for factual accuracy in a warrant.
Answer: A
Authorities: Reasonableness of warrant execution; factual-error analysis
Explanation: Factual errors in a warrant are assessed for reasonableness based on what officers knew at the time, not evaluated with the benefit of hindsight; this is why a warrant can remain valid despite a later-discovered inaccuracy. B applies a strict hindsight standard the doctrine rejects, C invents a fraud requirement, and D goes too far by suggesting officers bear no responsibility at all for reasonable accuracy.

## ccp-02-q06
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Detention during warrant execution
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 4-5 (Aug. 26-31, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: medium
Type: mc
Title: Question 6
Prompt: While executing a validly issued search warrant for a residence, officers detain an occupant who is on the premises, without any individualized suspicion that this particular occupant is dangerous or involved in criminal activity. Which statement best describes the constitutionality of this detention?
A: It is unconstitutional without individualized reasonable suspicion specific to that occupant.
B: Officers executing a search warrant may detain occupants found on the premises during the search without individualized suspicion, given the law-enforcement interests in safety, orderly execution, and preventing flight.
C: It is constitutional only if the occupant is later found to possess contraband.
D: It is unconstitutional because detention always requires probable cause to arrest.
Answer: B
Authorities: Categorical detention authority during warrant execution
Explanation: Officers may detain occupants on the premises during a warrant's execution without any individualized suspicion, justified by minimizing the risk of harm, enabling orderly execution, and preventing flight if contraband is found. A and D impose suspicion or probable-cause requirements the categorical rule does not, and C improperly makes the detention's validity depend on the search's ultimate results.

## ccp-02-q07

Difficulty: hard

Title: Question 7
Prompt: Officers watch an occupant leave a residence just before executing a search warrant there, get into a car, and drive off. They follow and detain him about a mile away, relying solely on the warrant to justify the stop, with no other individualized suspicion. Which statement is correct?
A: The categorical authority to detain occupants during a warrant's execution extends anywhere the person travels afterward, since they were on the premises when the warrant issued.
B: The detention is automatically unlawful because the occupant had already left the premises before the warrant was executed.
C: The categorical detention authority is limited to the immediate vicinity of the premises; a detention this far away needs its own independent justification, such as individualized reasonable suspicion.
D: The detention is lawful only if officers can show the occupant would have destroyed evidence if not stopped.
Answer: C
Authorities: Immediate-vicinity limit on detention authority
Explanation: The categorical authority to detain occupants without individualized suspicion is confined to the immediate vicinity of the premises being searched; a detention a mile away, based on nothing but the warrant, needs its own independent justification. A extends the categorical rule too far, B treats timing alone as dispositive without addressing the vicinity question, and D invents an evidence-destruction showing the standard does not require.

## ccp-02-q08

Difficulty: hard

Title: Question 8
Prompt: While executing a search warrant for evidence at a residence, officers also pat down a social guest who happens to be present, based on nothing more than the guest's presence on the premises during the search. Which statement is correct?
A: A frisk is permitted only if the warrant specifically names the guest.
B: Mere presence on the premises during a warrant's execution is sufficient by itself to justify a frisk of anyone found there.
C: A frisk is never permitted during the execution of a search warrant for evidence, regardless of the circumstances.
D: A frisk of a person present during a search still requires individualized reasonable suspicion that the specific person is armed and dangerous; presence alone is not enough.
Answer: D
Authorities: Limits on searching persons present during a warrant's execution
Explanation: The authority to detain occupants during a search does not extend to frisking them; a frisk still requires individualized reasonable suspicion that the particular person is armed and dangerous. B conflates detention authority with the separate, more demanding frisk standard, A imposes an unnecessary naming requirement, and C overstates the limitation by ruling out frisks entirely rather than requiring individualized suspicion.

## ccp-02-q09
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I

Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 4-5 (Aug. 26-31, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 9
Prompt: Officers execute a valid warrant to search a residence reasonably believed to be connected to an armed and dangerous suspect. They handcuff an occupant found inside for several hours while securing the scene and completing the search. Which factor most directly supports the reasonableness of this extended, forceful detention?
A: The inherently dangerous circumstances reasonably believed to be present at the scene, which can justify a more restrictive and longer detention than an ordinary search would require.
B: The length of the detention alone, regardless of the underlying danger, automatically renders it unreasonable.
C: Handcuffing is per se unreasonable during any search, no matter the circumstances.
D: The detention is reasonable only if the occupant is ultimately charged with a crime.
Answer: A
Authorities: Reasonableness of restrictive detention in dangerous circumstances
Explanation: When officers reasonably believe the scene presents genuine danger, a longer and more restrictive detention, including handcuffing, can be reasonable as part of securing the search; reasonableness is assessed against the circumstances known at the time, not a fixed durational cap. B treats duration as automatically dispositive, C adopts a categorical rule against any restraint, and D wrongly conditions reasonableness on the eventual outcome of the investigation.

## ccp-02-q10
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Time limits on forcing entry
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 4-5 (Aug. 26-31, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 10
Prompt: Officers knock and announce their presence and purpose at an apartment believed to contain a small quantity of a powdered controlled substance. After waiting 15 to 20 seconds with no response, they force the door open. The occupant was in the shower and did not hear them. Which analysis is correct?
A: The wait was unreasonable as a matter of law, because 15 to 20 seconds is never enough time for anyone to answer a door.
B: The wait's reasonableness turns on the risk that easily disposable evidence could be destroyed once occupants realize police are present, not on how long it would take a resident to physically walk to the door or on facts about what the occupant subjectively knew.
C: The entry was automatically unreasonable because the occupant genuinely did not hear the officers.
D: The wait must always be at least sixty seconds regardless of what type of evidence is being sought.
Answer: B
Authorities: Reasonableness of wait time before forced entry
Explanation: Once officers knock and announce in a case involving easily disposable evidence, the reasonable wait time is measured against the risk of disposal, not against how long it would take a resident to reach the door, and the occupant's subjective failure to hear the officers does not control since reasonableness is judged by what officers knew at the time. A and D impose rigid numeric floors the doctrine rejects, and C misapplies a subjective standard the analysis does not use.

## ccp-02-q11
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I

Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: medium

Title: Question 11
Prompt: Officers knock and announce at a home to execute a warrant for a large piece of stolen furniture too big to be quickly hidden or destroyed. After waiting only a few seconds with no answer, they force entry. Which principle is most relevant to evaluating this wait time?
A: The same short wait time that applies to disposable evidence like drugs applies equally to bulky, non-disposable items.
B: Officers may force entry immediately regardless of what is being sought, so long as the underlying warrant is valid.
C: Because the evidence sought is not easily disposable, officers generally must exercise more patience and wait longer before forcing entry than they would when evidence could be quickly destroyed.
D: Non-disposable evidence removes any knock-and-announce obligation entirely.
Answer: C
Authorities: Non-disposable-evidence contrast on wait-time reasonableness
Explanation: When the evidence sought is not easily disposable, the urgency that justifies a very short wait for drugs or similar evidence is absent, so officers are generally expected to wait longer and exercise more patience before forcing entry. A ignores that the disposal risk is precisely what varies between the two situations, B would eliminate the wait-time inquiry entirely, and D confuses the type of evidence sought with whether the knock-and-announce requirement applies at all.

## ccp-02-q12
Course: Constitutional Criminal Procedure I
Topic: Historical cell-site data and the third-party doctrine
Set: CCP-02
Coverage: Classes 1-3 (Aug. 17-24, 2026)
Theme: Larkspur Crossing
Difficulty: hard
Type: mc
Title: Question 12
Prompt: The government compels a wireless carrier to disclose months of historical records showing which cell towers a suspect's phone connected to, without a warrant, relying on the same reasoning that defeated privacy claims in cases involving dialed-number records and bank records. Which distinction most undermines that reliance?
A: There is no meaningful distinction; historical cell-tower records are conveyed to a carrier exactly as dialed numbers are conveyed to a phone company.
B: The suspect could simply have chosen not to own a phone, which fully justifies treating the records as voluntarily conveyed.
C: The distinction turns entirely on whether the carrier charged the suspect a fee for the data plan.
D: Unlike the earlier records, this data is comprehensive enough to reconstruct a person's whole pattern of movement over time, is generated automatically by the network rather than through any voluntary act of the user, and was not meaningfully limited in quantity or quality the way the earlier records were.
Answer: D
Authorities: Distinctions between historical location data and the classic third-party doctrine
Explanation: What separates this kind of comprehensive location data from the records in earlier third-party-doctrine cases is its capacity to reveal a detailed, continuous chronicle of movement, its automatic generation by the network rather than a voluntary user act, and the far greater quantity and sensitivity of what is conveyed. A denies a real and significant distinction, B revives a rejected assumption-of-risk argument in an extreme form, and C fixates on an irrelevant billing detail.
